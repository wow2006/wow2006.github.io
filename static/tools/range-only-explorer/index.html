Find the robot from its distances to beacons

Drag the robot and the beacons. Each pink circle is one measured distance; the shading is the cost F, from low to high as in the legend; the green star is where least squares puts the robot.

The cost is F(p) = ½ Σ (|p − bᵢ| − zᵢ)². Row i of the Jacobian J is the unit vector from beacon i to p: it says which way to move to grow that distance fastest.

  1. Look at the pale band around the star: a long, curved valley. The beacons all sit in one direction, so moving toward them changes every distance, but moving sideways, along the circles, changes almost none.
  2. Tick “Show the rows of J”. The blue arrows point almost the same way. The dashed orange line is the direction they do not see: the floor of the valley. κ(JᵀJ) says how much flatter it is.
  3. Press “Beacons all around”. The arrows now point every way, the valley becomes a round bowl, and κ drops close to 1.
  4. Press “2 beacons”. Two circles cross at two points, so there are two stars: two perfect answers, and the cost cannot tell them apart.
  5. Raise the noise. The circles stop meeting in one point, and the star moves away from the true robot.
